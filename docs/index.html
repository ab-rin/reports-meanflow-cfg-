02 / A QUESTION

Lower FID. Better images?

We were being evaluated on FID, so I spent a lot of time getting it lower. The part I found counterintuitive was going below the distance between the real training and validation data. If those sets are already different, what does beating that number mean?

The course reports 20.82 for the train–validation gap. Its November 5 update gives full base credit at 22 and below. I wanted to compare a checkpoint around 22 with the one I had spent much longer optimizing.

I checked the baseline again. With 20 real training images per class, it was 20.86 ± 0.35 across three subsets. Using the full training split gave 15.71. The reference itself moves when the protocol changes.

These local checks use the same clean-fid features, but do not reconstruct the course’s exact baseline protocol. Subset results · Full protocol

I would not call 20.82 a hard floor. FID compares feature statistics, and finite samples introduce bias. A score below it is possible; it does not prove that a model has become worse. It also does not tell me, by itself, that the extra training was worth it.

So here are the images. Look at them before the numbers.

04 / METHOD

MeanFlow, with guidance built in.

MeanFlow learns the average velocity over a time interval. I adapted the class-conditional U-Net and training setup to the Pokémon task, with classifier-free guidance in the training target. At inference, each interval needs one conditional network call.

The method comes from Geng et al.; the backbone comes from Gsunshine/py-meanflow. My work here is the implementation for this task, training, and experiments.

xr = xt − (t − r) uθ(xt, t, r, c)
THE TRAINING LOOP
image + noisemix at time t
↓
class-conditioned U-Netpredict average velocity

↓
Adam + EMAsample with the averaged weights
Training recipe and objective

zt = (1 − t)x + tε. Labeled examples use vg = 2(ε − x) − uθ(zt, t, t, ∅); dropped labels use ε − x.

The target is stopgrad[vg − (t − r)D uθ]. The JVP follows vg in space and increases t while holding r fixed. The adaptive loss exponent is 0.75.
